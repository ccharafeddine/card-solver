Why it works

Every blackjack choice has an exact expected value (EV): what it wins or loses on average per unit bet. The app computes it from the cards left in the shoe, card by card for the dealer. Basic strategy is just the choice with the highest EV.

The house edge comes from one rule: if you bust, you lose even if the dealer busts later. Good play cuts the edge to under half a percent with good rules. Counting finds the moments when the leftover cards favour you.

Kuhn poker: poker with 3 cards

Exact

The smallest real poker game, small enough to solve perfectly. The deck is just a Jack, a Queen and a King (King beats Queen beats Jack). Two players each get one card and put 1 chip in the pot.

  1. Player 1 checks (bets nothing) or bets 1 chip.
  2. If player 1 bet, player 2 calls (puts in 1 too) or folds (gives up the pot).
  3. If player 1 checked, player 2 checks (show cards, higher wins) or bets, and then player 1 calls or folds.

Mathematicians found the perfect way to play it in 1950. It shows why even perfect players bluff.

The perfect strategy

Nash equilibrium

How often to do each thing with each card. Nobody can win more against it by playing differently.

Any rate from 0% to 33% is perfect, as long as player 1 also bets the King 3 times as often and calls with the Queen a bit more.

Why bluffing works

If you only bet with good cards, your bets give your hand away and nobody pays you. Bluffing sometimes with your worst card keeps the other player guessing, so they have to call sometimes, and then your King gets paid.

Best hold

Exact

How it works

There are 32 ways to choose what to hold (including keeping nothing). For each one the app deals every possible replacement from the 47 cards left, up to 1.5 million of them, adds up the payouts and divides. The hold with the highest average is the best play.

Surprises it finds: keep a small pair over three cards to a straight flush, and throw away a flush you've already made when you hold four to a royal.

What to do

Heuristic

Deadwood is exact. Which card to throw, whether to take the face-up card, and when to knock use rules of thumb good players use; gin rummy is too big to solve perfectly here.

How gin rummy works

Each player has 10 cards. On your turn, take the face-up card or the top of the pile, then throw one away. You're trying to group your cards into melds: sets (3 or 4 of a rank, like 9 9 9) or runs (3 or more in a row in one suit, like 4♥ 5♥ 6♥).

Cards not in a meld are deadwood and count against you (aces 1, pictures 10). With 10 or less you can knock to end the hand; with 0 you go gin for a bonus.

How cribbage scoring works

You're dealt 6 cards and throw 2 into the crib, a bonus hand for the dealer. Then a card is cut (the starter) and shared by everyone. Your 4 cards plus the cut score points for fifteens (cards adding to 15), pairs, runs (3 or more in a row), flushes and nobs (the jack of the cut's suit).

Pick the six cards you were dealt, or press Deal.

Best throw

Exact average

Hand: the exact average over all 46 possible cut cards. Crib: the exact average over every 2 cards the other player could throw and every cut, treating their throw as random (real players throw better, so treat it as a guide). As dealer the crib is yours; otherwise it counts against you.

Play responsibly

This app is for learning and fun. No strategy beats the house over time without counting, and even then the edge is small and swings are large. Only gamble with money you can afford to lose, set limits before you play, and never chase losses. If gambling stops being fun, talk to someone: in the US call or text 1-800-GAMBLER; elsewhere, look for a local gambling helpline.
